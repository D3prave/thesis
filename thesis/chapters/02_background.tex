\chapter{Background}

Large language models routinely produce fluent answers that are not supported
by the facts of the task. Such failures are typically grouped under the broad
label of ``hallucination'', although the term spans several distinct
mechanisms: unfaithfulness to source material, unsupported factual claims,
mistakes caused by gaps in the model's knowledge, systematic reasoning
errors, and answers encouraged by evaluation incentives that reward guessing
over abstention \cite{ji_hallucination_survey_2023,
huang_llm_hallucination_survey_2023,maynez_faithfulness_factuality_2020,
kalai_why_language_models_hallucinate_2025,augenstein_factuality_challenges_2024}.
This thesis adopts the broader vocabulary where it is convenient, but its
target is narrower: detecting when an answer is unreliable because the model
is uncertain about the meaning of the answer it has produced.

\section{Hallucinations and Confabulations}

The semantic entropy paper sharpens this target to \emph{confabulations}:
fluent answers that are both incorrect and arbitrary, where arbitrary means
that the answer is sensitive to irrelevant variation such as the random seed
used during decoding \cite{semantic_entropy_nature_2024}. The practical
stakes are not hypothetical: controlled studies of LLMs in clinical
decision-support settings find hallucination rates of 50--82\,\% when
fabricated details are embedded in prompts, and prompt-based mitigation
reduces but does not eliminate the problem \cite{omar_adversarial_hallucination_clinical_2025}.
The distinction between confabulation and other hallucination types matters
because it bounds what semantic entropy can and cannot detect. A model may
also be consistently wrong because its training data contains a
misconception, because the problem requires reasoning it cannot perform, or
because evaluation incentives discourage abstention. Semantic entropy is not
designed to address any of these cases; it is a detector for semantic
instability across alternative generations from the same prompt.

This narrower framing motivates the central hypothesis of the thesis. If the
model knows the answer, stochastic samples should vary mostly in wording
while preserving meaning. If the model is confabulating, the samples should
split across several incompatible meanings. Measuring uncertainty over
meanings rather than over surface strings should therefore be a more
informative signal for free-form question answering
\cite{kuhn_semantic_uncertainty_2023,semantic_entropy_nature_2024}. The
corresponding limitation must be stated alongside the hypothesis:
uncertainty-based detection is, by construction, unable to flag
high-certainty hallucinations or systematic errors
\cite{simhi_choke_2025,ma_semantic_energy_2025}.

\section{Uncertainty in Free-Form Generation}

For a classifier with a fixed label set, uncertainty estimation is
well-defined and the categorical predictive distribution supplies the
relevant signal. Language generation does not admit such a clean definition,
because the output space is open-ended: many different strings can express
the same proposition, and superficially similar strings can express
different facts. Sequence-level entropy or token-level variation can
therefore be misleading on natural-language answers
\cite{baan_uncertainty_nlg_2023,kuhn_semantic_uncertainty_2023}. The thesis
distinguishes three levels of variation accordingly:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item lexical variation: different token sequences or paraphrases;
  \item semantic variation: different meanings or propositions;
  \item factual correctness: whether the chosen meaning is correct for the
    task.
\end{enumerate}

Semantic entropy operates at the second level. It estimates uncertainty
over semantic answers, not over truth directly; correctness evaluation is a
separate step that compares the selected answer against a reference or a
judge \cite{semantic_entropy_nature_2024}. Recent surveys place this family
of methods within uncertainty quantification for LLMs, alongside
calibration, verbalized confidence, internal-state methods, and other
sampling-based estimators \cite{shorinwa_uq_llm_survey_2024,
liu_uq_confidence_calibration_survey_2025}.

\section{Confidence, Calibration, and Abstention}

The motivation for estimating uncertainty is not only to attach a score to
an answer, but to decide what should happen next: answer, abstain, defer to
retrieval, request human review, or invoke a more expensive verification
procedure. This makes selective prediction and rejection-based evaluation
the natural evaluation lens for the thesis. Accuracy alone is insufficient,
because many benchmarks reward a lucky guess more than an honest admission
of uncertainty \cite{kalai_why_language_models_hallucinate_2025}.
Rejection-accuracy curves and AURAC are better aligned with the practical
question of whether uncertain answers can be filtered out. Related work has
also examined whether semantic entropy can be used as a training signal to
encourage abstention, but the present thesis treats that line as a
downstream mitigation rather than a primary target
\cite{tjandra_abstain_semantic_entropy_2024}.

Several baselines approach the same problem without semantic clustering.
P(True) asks the model to judge whether its own answer is correct, probing
verbalized self-knowledge rather than sample diversity
\cite{kadavath_language_models_know_2022}. SelfCheckGPT instead samples
multiple responses and checks them for consistency, without relying on
external databases or access to token probabilities
\cite{manakul_selfcheckgpt_2023}. Semantic entropy is closest in spirit to
these sampling-based methods, but, unlike them, it explicitly aggregates
samples into semantic clusters before computing an entropy score
\cite{kuhn_semantic_uncertainty_2023,semantic_entropy_nature_2024}.

\section{Semantic Equivalence and Entailment}

The key operational step is deciding when two sampled answers should be
treated as the same answer. Semantic entropy adopts semantic equivalence
rather than exact string equality: in the original formulation, two answers
belong to the same group when they entail one another in the context of the
question \cite{semantic_entropy_nature_2024}. This bidirectional-entailment
criterion is a practical approximation to equality of meaning.

Natural Language Inference (NLI) supplies a local way to approximate this
relation, because an NLI model assigns one of three labels --- entailment,
contradiction, or neutrality --- to a pair of sentences
\cite{williams_multinli_2018}. DeBERTa-style NLI models are the most common
local backend in the literature \cite{he_deberta_2021}. For the background,
the relevant point is that semantic equivalence is being approximated; the
exact bidirectional-entailment rule and the choice of pilot backend are
settled in Chapter 3.

\section{Extensions and Related Methods}

Several recent methods extend or challenge the hard-cluster formulation of
semantic entropy. Kernel Language Entropy, Semantic Energy, Semantic
Volume, adaptive Bayesian semantic entropy, and semantically diverse
generation all ask whether semantic uncertainty can be estimated more
flexibly, more cheaply, or with richer representations. Kernel-, energy-,
and volume-based methods are the principal high-compute comparators
\cite{nikitin_kernel_language_entropy_2024,ma_semantic_energy_2025,
li_semantic_volume_2025}. Adaptive and diversity-driven sampling methods
become relevant when the cost of estimating uncertainty is itself the
bottleneck \cite{sun_adaptive_bayesian_semantic_entropy_2026,
aichberger_semantically_diverse_generation_2025}. In the thesis these
methods sit at the Phase 2 boundary defined in Chapter 7 and only enter the
primary comparison if the discrete baseline proves stable.

A separate line of work uses internal model states. Probing studies suggest
that hidden activations carry information about truthfulness and
hallucination risk \cite{burns_latent_knowledge_2023,
azaria_internal_state_lying_2023,chen_inside_2024}. This evidence
motivates Semantic Entropy Probes, but the probe setup is not part of the
background argument and is deferred to Chapter 7
\cite{kossen_semantic_entropy_probes_2024}. Neuron-level hallucination
work is relevant to the wider discussion as well, although it shifts the
problem from black-box uncertainty estimation toward white-box
interpretability and intervention \cite{gao_h_neurons_2025}.

\section{Evaluation Setting}

The first experimental setting is sentence-length question answering rather
than paragraph-length biography verification. This restriction keeps the
pipeline focused on its four core steps: sampling answers, clustering
meanings, scoring uncertainty, and measuring whether uncertainty predicts
incorrectness. TriviaQA provides an open-domain question-answering
benchmark with short factual answers \cite{joshi_triviaqa_2017}, and SVAMP
provides math word problems in which equivalent numerical answers and
concise reasoning matter \cite{patel_svamp_2021}. SQuAD, BioASQ, and
NQ-Open are useful subsequent expansions because they appear in the wider
semantic entropy evaluation setting \cite{rajpurkar_squad_2016,
tsatsaronis_bioasq_2015,lee_nq_open_2019}. TruthfulQA is relevant to the
broader truthfulness problem but is not the first pilot benchmark, because
the initial experiment is intended to isolate semantic clustering on
short-answer tasks \cite{lin_truthfulqa_2022}. Where the experiments use
LLaMA 2 or Mistral models, their respective model reports are cited as
part of the implementation context \cite{touvron_llama2_2023,
jiang_mistral_2023}. The released semantic uncertainty codebase serves as a
methodological reference against which implementation decisions can be
checked \cite{kossen_semantic_uncertainty_code_2024}.

\section{Research Gap}

Taken together, the literature positions semantic entropy as a middle
ground between cheap surface-level uncertainty signals and expensive
factual verification. Its value depends on a clean separation between
generation, semantic clustering, entropy scoring, correctness labeling, and
selective-prediction metrics. The thesis therefore begins with a small,
reproducible implementation of discrete semantic entropy before turning to
more ambitious extensions such as Semantic Entropy Probes or kernel-based
variants. Chapter 3 formalizes this restricted method; the experimental
design and HPC chapters then specify how it will be tested in the two-phase
study.
