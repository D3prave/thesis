\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This thesis asked whether semantic entropy provides a better
hallucination-risk and abstention signal than surface-form uncertainty,
and how that answer depends on the surface-form variability of the
task, the clustering backend, and the scale of the generator. The
method follows Farquhar et al.~\cite{semantic_entropy_nature_2024};
the contribution here is a reproducible reimplementation evaluated on
a full cross-seed grid (three open generators, three short-answer
datasets and a long-form biography task, three decoding seeds under
the original paper's sampling and labeling protocol) plus a
claim-level long-form extension, rather than a new semantic-entropy
method.

The answer is regime-dependent, in both directions and more sharply
than expected. On short-answer tasks the core count-based sampling estimators
generally reach AUROC values of roughly $0.70$--$0.90$, but entropy over
normalized strings, the cheapest score in the family, is the strongest of
the three count-based sampling scores in eight of nine model--dataset cells
and the strongest of all detectors in six of them (outright in five, tied in
one), and no semantic estimator
beats it by a practically relevant margin anywhere: every mean difference
for discrete semantic entropy is negative, the single exception in the whole
family is a $0.002$ AUROC lead for the probability-weighted variant on
SVAMP with Llama-3.1-70B, and the only Holm-significant result favors the
surface baseline. A record-level
analysis explains why: dataset normalization already removes almost all
surface variation before any semantic step runs, so clustering has little
left to merge. On long-form text the two count-based string-frequency scores
have little to measure: paragraphs almost never repeat as identical strings,
so the scores are nearly pinned at their ceiling and sit near chance.
Surface-only probability-weighted naive entropy remains strong, while
meaning-level clustering and a correctness-trained probe recover signal by
different routes.
Measured at claim level against human factuality labels, semantic entropy
detects confabulated claims at AUROC 0.748, 0.724 and 0.701 for the three
generators. On whole paragraphs the picture is weaker and, on the evidence
of two independent diagnostics, partly an artifact of granularity rather
than of the method: the score is pinned at its ceiling for a large minority
of records, and one generator's paragraphs are frequently cut off at the
decoding limit.

Two further findings qualify the method's practical use. First, the
clustering backend is result-determining on long-form inputs: across NLI
cross-encoders and an LLM judge, backends span anti-predictive to
moderately useful behavior on the same saved samples, while on short answers
the same four backends span at most 0.057 AUROC. Backend capacity matters in
proportion to the length of the text being compared, which makes it an
implementation detail on short answers and a first-order design decision on
long-form text. Second, the claim-level FactualBio extension shows that
semantic entropy's discriminative power decreases monotonically with
generator scale (AUROC 0.748 $\rightarrow$ 0.724 $\rightarrow$ 0.701 from 7B
to 70B), a pattern consistent with larger models repeating their
confabulations more consistently and thereby leaving less inconsistency to
measure. The experiment does not establish that mechanism directly. The effect is a
gentle slope rather than a collapse --- 0.047 AUROC across a tenfold size
difference, with the largest model still clearly useful --- and it should not
be extrapolated beyond the three models measured here.
Hidden-state probes offer a cheaper, single-pass alternative. On short
answers the entropy-trained SEP and correctness-trained accuracy probe land
close together, with the correctness target ahead in eight of nine held-out
cells. The whole-paragraph biography design reports only the accuracy probe,
at $0.704 / 0.680 / 0.773$ from 7B to 70B; it defines neither a genuine SEP nor
an OOD transfer result. Its scale pattern is not monotonic, so those three
cells give no evidence that hidden-state detection strengthens with model
size. The measured conclusion is narrower: the correctness-trained probe remains a
useful single-pass comparator when whole-paragraph cluster scores saturate.

\section{Contributions}

The substantive method comes from Farquhar et
al.~\cite{semantic_entropy_nature_2024}. The contributions promised in
Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} were delivered as six artifacts:
(1)~a reproducible \texttt{semantic\_entropy} implementation
(Chapters~\ref{ch:semantic_entropy}--\ref{ch:experimental_design});
(2)~the complete cross-seed experiment record with paired-bootstrap
significance kept separate from seed stability
(Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation-metrics}--\ref{sec:hpc-execution});
(3)~the extension baselines --- P(True), in- and out-of-distribution
embedding regression, Kernel Language Entropy under two encoders, and
the Semantic Entropy Probe~\cite{kossen_semantic_entropy_probes_2024},
with the observation that correctness-trained and SE-trained probes perform
similarly in this grid, although the correctness-trained probe is ahead in
eight of nine cells
(Sections~\ref{sec:related-estimators}, \ref{sec:probes-results});
(4)~the two-granularity long-form evaluation, whole-paragraph and
claim-level, which yields the scale trend, the clustering-artifact result,
and evidence from this biography task that a consistency-based detector should
be evaluated at a granularity where independent samples can plausibly agree, a
condition that can be checked from the samples before any AUROC is computed
(Sections~\ref{sec:bio-results}, \ref{sec:factualbio-results});
(5)~the record-level worked audits that give mechanistic rather than
purely metric-level evidence for the main conclusions
(Section~\ref{sec:hand-audits}); and (6)~the sampling-temperature sweep,
which isolates the steep temperature dependence of the sampling-based
family against a near-flat single-pass baseline, and shows the resulting
crossing to depend on model scale
(Section~\ref{sec:temperature-sensitivity}).

The full source code, result tables, Slurm templates, and LaTeX thesis
source are available at \url{https://github.com/D3prave/thesis}.
The Python implementation lives in the \texttt{src/semantic\_entropy/}
package; entry points are documented in \texttt{README.md}.

\section{Limitations}

The thesis is deliberately narrow in scope. The detection target is the
confabulation setting defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:background}.
High-confidence systematic errors are by construction not detected by
this method, a limitation the FactualBio scale trend makes concrete,
since consistent confabulation at 70B is precisely the confident-error
case; Simhi et al.~\cite{simhi_choke_2025} discuss this setting.

Within the empirical results, the main residual limitations are the
automated LLM judging behind the whole-paragraph long-form labels (the
claim-level evaluation uses published human labels), the three-seed basis of
the stability estimates, and the English-only, biography-only long-form
scope. The hidden-state probes were not included in the temperature sweep, the
granularity interpretation comes from one long-form task, and AUROC ranking
alone does not establish deployment utility. The claim-level evaluation was
also run without a surface comparator, so it shows that meaning-based
scoring works at claim granularity but does not by itself separate
meaning-aware from count-based scoring; that separation rests on the
whole-paragraph comparison, which
Section~\ref{sec:bio-results} treats as a diagnostic.
Record-level issues (alias-type oracle failures on TriviaQA,
semantically consistent refusals on biography, and SVAMP's
integer-arithmetic structure) are documented in the audits; the
fuller discussion is in Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations}.
